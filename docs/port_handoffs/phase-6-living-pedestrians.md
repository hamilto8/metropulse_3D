Topology and lifecycle: The graph contains 240 grid sidewalk nodes plus six park-path nodes. River gaps connect only through the two authored near-zero bridge walkways; every node is bidirectional and routable. The population starts and remains at 60 monotonic `pedestrian-NNNN` identities. Culling retires an ID and immediately replaces it; supported recovery rejoins the nearest sidewalk node without changing identity.

Special behavior: Cafe readers occupy the six canonical seats and do not enter traffic queries. Tourists alternate walking and deterministic photo pauses. Joggers use their authored speed/mode. Criminals use bounded spatial queries, single-target reservations, chase duration/radius, non-graphic knockdown, and reservation cleanup. Knocked-down agents retain identity and bounded recovery state.

Traffic interaction and pursuit: Vehicle detection distance includes current speed, reaction time, braking distance, and clearance. Patient drivers remain stopped; the deterministic 20% impatient cohort waits 3.5 seconds, horns exactly once, then proceeds. Large vehicles use the 4.2-metre emergency threshold; others use 3.1 metres. NPC impacts begin hit-and-run state unless the offender is police, player-controlled, destroyed, or already fleeing. A 65-metre grid query selects at most two available police by distance then stable ID. Fugitives use a 1.55 speed multiplier capped at 38; police catch up by 8 capped at 42 and select the next road edge closest to the offender. At 22 seconds, damage, cull, or invalid assignment, offender/responders clear to normal patrol and sirens disable.

Simulation/render cadence: Near/medium/far pedestrian simulation runs every 1/2/8 ticks at 130/340 metres; only near healthy standing agents have physical collision. Render LOD independently uses full meshes with shadows, full meshes without shadows, and low capsule proxies at 120/320 metres. Identity and special-behavior state persist at every tier.
